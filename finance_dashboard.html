Problem Case
Think Before Text · Activate Prior Knowledge
Case: Company Performance Problem

A company reports high revenue, but investors are not satisfied. The financial report shows unclear results.

Task 1 — Discussion Questions
Think about each question before writing. Use financial logic and your own knowledge.
Question 1
❓ Why is revenue not enough to evaluate a company's performance?
💡 Hint: Consider expenses, profit, and cash flow. High revenue with high costs = low or negative profit.
Question 2
❓ What information should investors analyze besides revenue?
💡 Hint: Think about profit margins, cash flow, liabilities, equity, and financial ratios.
Question 3
❓ What risks can appear if financial reports are incorrect? Give 2–3 arguments and predict consequences.
💡 Hint: Consider investor trust, legal consequences, poor decisions, market instability.

Finance and Financial Reporting
Main Text · Hover highlighted terms for definitions
📌 Hover over highlighted termsThis is how tooltips work! to see definitions. Each term links to the glossary.

Financial reportingThe process of producing financial statements that disclose an organization's financial status. is a key element of business activity. It provides information about a company's financial positionA snapshot of what a company owns (assets) and owes (liabilities) at a specific point in time. and performance. ManagersPeople responsible for controlling or administering an organization., investorsPeople or organizations that provide capital expecting a financial return., and regulators use this information to evaluateTo assess or judge the value, quality or performance of something. how effectively the company operates and whether it is financially stableFinancially stable = consistent, not prone to large fluctuations in performance.. Without reliableReliable = consistently good in quality; can be trusted to be accurate. financial reporting, it is difficult to make informed decisionsDecisions made with sufficient knowledge and understanding of the situation..

The main financial statementsFormal records of financial activities — typically income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. present different aspects of a company's activity. The income statementA financial statement showing revenues, expenses, and profit over a period of time. Also called P&L. shows revenueThe total income generated by a company from its business activities before expenses., expenses, and profit, helping to understand whether the company is profitableGenerating more revenue than expenses; yielding financial gain.. The balance sheetA financial statement showing assets, liabilities and equity at a specific date. presents assetsResources owned or controlled by a company that have economic value., liabilitiesFinancial obligations or debts owed by a company to others., and equityThe residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities; owners' stake., which reflect the company's financial structure. The cash flow statementA financial statement showing how cash moves in and out of a business. explains how money moves in and out of the business, showing whether the company has enough liquidityThe ability to meet short-term financial obligations; how easily assets can be converted to cash. to operate.

However, financial data must be analyzed carefully. High revenueTotal income from sales and services before any deductions. does not always mean strong performanceAchieving significantly good financial and operational results.. If expenses are also high, profit may be low or even negative. Financial analysisThe process of evaluating financial data to understand performance and make decisions. helps identify inefficienciesFailure to make the best use of resources; wasteful processes in a business., risks, and potential problems. It allows managers to improve operations and helps investors evaluate whether the company is a good investment.

Accuracy and transparencyOpenness and honesty in financial reporting; disclosing all relevant information clearly. are essential in financial reporting. Incorrect or incompleteNot having all necessary parts; missing important information. information can misleadTo cause someone to have a wrong idea or impression about something. users and lead to poor decisionsChoices made without sufficient or accurate information, leading to negative outcomes.. Companies must follow accounting standardsTo comply with official rules governing financial record-keeping (e.g. IFRS, GAAP). and provide clear and reliable data. Transparent reporting increases trust and reduces financial risk for investors and partners.

Financial reporting is directly connected to decision-makingThe process of making choices based on available information and analysis.. Managers use financial data to plan budgets, control costs, and develop strategies. Investors rely onTo depend on something as a source of support or information. reports to assess risks and expected returns. Therefore, high-quality financial reporting is not only a formal requirement but also a strategic tool for business development.
